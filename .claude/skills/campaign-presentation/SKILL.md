# Campaign Presentation Skill

Build compelling, structured marketing presentations — from campaign proposals to performance read-outs — ready for stakeholder review.

## Workflow

Make a todo list for all the tasks in this workflow and work on them one after another.

### 1. Clarify Presentation Goals

Ask the user for the following if not already provided:
- **Presentation type:** Campaign proposal, strategy overview, performance review, investor update, post-mortem, creative review
- **Audience:** C-suite, marketing team, board, client, agency, investors
- **Objective:** Approve budget, align on strategy, share results, gain buy-in
- **Campaign or initiative being presented**
- **Key materials available:** ICP, strategy doc, creative assets, data/results
- **Desired length:** Number of slides or time limit (e.g., 10 slides, 20-minute presentation)
- **Tone:** Formal, conversational, bold, data-heavy, story-driven

### 2. Define Presentation Narrative

Build a clear story arc:
- **Opening Hook:** Start with a provocative insight, bold claim, or audience-relevant question
- **Context & Problem:** Set up why this matters now
- **Strategy / Solution:** What you did or propose to do
- **Evidence / Results:** Data, creative examples, validation
- **Ask / Next Steps:** What you need from the audience

### 3. Slide-by-Slide Outline

Build a detailed slide outline. For each slide, document:

```
Slide [#]: [Title]
Purpose: [What this slide accomplishes in the story]
Key Message: [One-sentence takeaway]
Content: [Bullets, data, visuals, quotes]
Visual Direction: [Chart type, image, layout suggestion]
Speaker Notes: [What to say out loud]
```

**Standard Campaign Proposal Structure:**
1. Cover Slide (Campaign name, date, presenter)
2. Executive Summary (TL;DR — 3 bullets max)
3. Situation & Opportunity (Market context, why now)
4. Target Audience (ICP summary, segments)
5. Campaign Objective & KPIs (SMART goals)
6. Strategy & Approach (Positioning, key message)
7. Channel Mix & Tactics (What, where, how)
8. Creative Direction (Concepts, visuals, copy samples)
9. Timeline & Milestones (Phased rollout Gantt or table)
10. Budget Breakdown (Allocation by channel, total investment)
11. Expected Outcomes (Projected results vs. investment)
12. Risk & Mitigation (What could go wrong and contingencies)
13. Next Steps & Ask (Decision needed, owners, deadlines)

**Standard Performance Review Structure:**
1. Cover Slide
2. Campaign Overview (Objectives, timeline, budget)
3. Executive Summary (Top wins and misses)
4. Performance vs. Goals (KPI scorecard)
5. Channel Performance (Breakdown by channel)
6. Creative Performance (Top/bottom performers)
7. Audience Insights (Who responded, who didn't)
8. Key Learnings (What worked, what didn't, why)
9. Recommendations (What to optimize or change)
10. Next Steps

### 4. Write Full Slide Content

For each slide, write:
- **Headline:** Action-oriented, insight-driven (not generic)
- **Body content:** Bullets (max 3–5 per slide), data callouts, quotes
- **Data visualization guidance:** Specify chart type and data to show
- **Speaker notes:** Natural-language script for presenting the slide

**Headline writing principles:**
- Bad: "Campaign Results"
- Good: "Email drove 3x more MQLs than paid social at half the cost"

### 5. Data & Visuals Guidance

For each data slide:
- Specify the exact metric and comparison (vs. target, vs. previous period)
- Recommend visualization type (bar chart, line graph, scorecard, funnel)
- Call out the "so what" interpretation explicitly

### 6. Appendix

Include supporting slides:
- Detailed data tables
- Methodology notes
- Additional creative examples
- Glossary of terms (if needed for mixed audience)

## Output Format

Deliver the presentation as:
1. **Slide-by-slide document** (markdown, with each slide clearly structured)
2. **Speaker notes** (included per slide)
3. **Executive summary** (1-page version of the full deck)

If the user has Google Slides, PowerPoint, or Canva, note which sections map to which template sections.

## Wrap Up

Summarize what was created. Offer to:
- Refine specific slides
- Add or remove slides based on audience feedback
- Create a shorter "leave-behind" one-pager version
- Pull in data from the data-analysis skill to populate performance slides
